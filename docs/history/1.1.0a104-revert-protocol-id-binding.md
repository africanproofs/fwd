## Why a103 is reverted

a103 bound `PROTOCOL_PAYLOAD` to an `allowed_protocol_ids` allowlist and made `protocol_id` required,
claiming it "bounded the opaque-payload signing surface." Grounding showed this was wrong at the root:

1. **The protocol-id is not in the signed bytes.** The fork signs `TextHash(Keccak256(data.Data))`,
   where `data.Data` is the inner subprotocol message. The protocol-id byte is written to the *outer*
   `submitSignatures` calldata (`submitter.go`: `tempBuffer.WriteByte(protocolID)`), which fwd never
   sees. So fwd cannot verify the declared `protocol_id` against the payload, and since `protocol_id`
   is not in the hash, the allowlist constrains **nothing about what gets signed** — it is audit
   metadata, not a signing bound. The binding was security theater.
2. **It broke the consumer contract.** Making `protocol_id` *required* diverged from `fwd-client/go`
   (which treats it optional) → a real PROTOCOL_PAYLOAD request from the existing Go client would now
   be rejected. a103 introduced client/server drift for zero security gain.
3. **It added a self-deny risk.** A misconfigured `allowed_protocol_ids` fails closed on healthy
   signing.

Net negative. Reverted before it was ever deployed (live fwd still runs `c916446`).

## The real status of the finding it tried to address

Finding #5 (opaque `PROTOCOL_PAYLOAD`) is **unaddressed by design**, and that is acceptable for the
asset class: fwd cannot semantically validate subprotocol payloads without embedding FTSO/FDC protocol
math (the documented compromise). The real bound is rate + audit + EIP-191 domain-separation (cannot
forge an EVM tx) + the consumer-is-trusted-for-content argument. This is now stated honestly rather
than papered over with a metadata allowlist.
