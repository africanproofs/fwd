## What `fwd` is (one paragraph context)

`fwd` is the Flare Wallet Daemon: a policy-gated signing service for African Proofs (AP). It exists for exactly one reason — to delete every `.env PRIVATE_KEY=` line across AP automation (`ftso-fee-claimer`, `apregister/`, `apcli`, future `fics` write paths, future autonomous agents) and replace each with one HTTP call to a service that decodes intent, enforces declarative default-deny policy, custodies keys envelope-encrypted, and keeps a hash-chained audit log. It is single-host, single-replica, single-operator, Docker-Compose deployed. HashiCorp Vault Transit (`aes256-gcm96`, `exportable=false`) envelope-encrypts externally-generated secp256k1 privkeys; `eth-account` signs in `fwd`'s own process post-decrypt. **Read `CLAUDE.md` § Identity, § Scope, § What FWD Deliberately IS NOT, and all 19 Core invariants before producing any finding.** The "IS NOT" list is the project's own anti-over-engineering charter — part of your job is to test whether the project lives up to it for the things it *did* build.

## Why this audit, and what it is not

This **is** the moment to challenge architecture and stack: Phase 7 GA is met, the substrate is feature-complete, and Phase 8 will custody real keys against these exact choices. Cuts are cheap now and expensive after.

This is **not** a re-run of the v0.3.1 / v0.4.0a1 custody audits. Their findings were closed (v0.3.2, v0.4.0a2). Do **not** re-litigate closed custody findings. You may *cite* a closed finding only where it bears on a stack or over-engineering judgment (e.g. "the v0.3.1 audit already conceded the Vault blast radius equals fwd's own — which sharpens the question of what the Vault container buys").

This is **not** a code-correctness or security review. Assume the code does what the tests say within the mocked envelope. Your lens is *proportionality and stack fit*, not bug-hunting — except where over-engineering itself creates risk (complexity that will make the Phase 8 migration more likely to lose a key is a Critical finding *in this audit's taxonomy*).

This is **not** a license to ignore that custody is high-stakes. Some ceremony is correct here. Your job is to draw the line between *correct ceremony* and *ceremony that has outrun the substrate it protects* — and to defend the line with evidence on both sides.

## What you must NOT mistake for over-engineering

- Things explicitly Phase-marked as deferred (Phase 8/9/10) are **scoped-out**, not over-built — UNLESS you find them already implemented and shipping in v1, in which case the question flips to "why was this pulled forward against the project's own simplicity doctrine?"
- Deliberate exclusions in § What FWD Deliberately IS NOT (no K8s, no web3.py, single replica, manual unseal v1, no public exposure) are *de-engineering* decisions. You may argue a specific one is wrong, but absence of a feature there is not a finding by itself.
- The test-to-code ratio and the live-drill discipline are not over-engineering on their face — v0.4.5 and v0.5.2 are the evidence they earn their keep. If you argue otherwise, you must explain what would have caught those two defects more cheaply.

You **may and should** challenge: the Vault dependency itself; the 19-invariant / D1–D16 / 42-history-doc / four-role-workflow apparatus relative to ~7k LOC and zero migrated keys; the async/concurrency machinery (FastAPI async, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, single-session `RequestScope`, 30s `busy_timeout`, the asyncio receipt-watcher) for a localhost low-QPS singleton; the receipt-watcher + replacement-on-stuck; idempotency replay; the full hash-chain tamper-evidence whose closing on-chain anchor is itself Phase-10-deferred; the ABI decoder's B1 projection generality; the admin CRUD surface; and the doctrine-only-version-bump practice. These are the sacred cows. They are in scope precisely because they are sacred.

## Report — required structure and the no-hedge rule

Write your report to **`docs/reviews/v0.5.4-stack-and-overengineering-audit-report.md`** (create it; this is the *only* file you write). Use the house severity taxonomy, recalibrated for this lens:

- **Critical** — a stack/architecture choice that will plausibly cause key loss or a failed/again-delayed Phase 8 migration if not addressed before it.
- **High** — significant over-engineering or stack misfit imposing ongoing cost with no offsetting need; or a simpler design exists that meets every invariant.
- **Medium** — disproportion worth correcting but not blocking.
- **Low** — minor; note and move on.
- **Observation** — neutral architectural note for the Reviewer's awareness.

Required sections, in this order:

0. **Executive verdict** — ≤ 8 sentences. Two explicit, decisive judgments, each on its scale, no hedging:
   - *Stack verdict:* `Right-sized` | `Mostly right, N components misfit` | `Over-heavy` — and the one sentence that matters most.
   - *Over-engineering verdict:* `Proportionate` | `Over-engineered in named areas` | `Process has become the product` — and the one sentence that matters most.
   A report whose executive verdict hedges either scale is **incomplete and will be rejected by the Reviewer.** Pick a position; defend it in the body.
1. **Stack assessment table** — one row per dependency/component: Component | Purpose | Earning its weight? (Yes / Partial / No) | Simplest alternative that meets the invariants | Recommendation | Severity.
2. **Over-engineering findings** — ranked `OE-1, OE-2, …`. Each: Claim · Evidence (file:line / doctrine ref / Phase-1 number) · Why disproportionate or speculative (tie to Behavioural guideline 2 where it applies) · Cost it imposes (on readers, ships, operations, Phase-8 risk) · **Steelman** (the strongest defense of the current design, stated fairly) · Net verdict · Action {cut now | defer to Phase N | keep & defend} · Severity.
3. **Under-engineering / gaps** — `UE-1, …`, same shape inverted. This audit cuts both ways: where has minimalism gone too far, or a real Phase-8 risk been left unaddressed because the energy went into ceremony elsewhere? A report with zero UE findings will be read as incomplete.
4. **Doctrine-to-code proportionality** — the explicit verdict from Phase 3, with the specific consolidation recommendations (or the specific evidence-backed defense that it is all load-bearing).
5. **Time-to-value** — your position on whether the pace and process serve "delete the `.env` lines," with the one change (if any) that would most accelerate real value without sacrificing the custody discipline that demonstrably matters.
6. **Prioritized recommendations for the Reviewer** — a numbered list, each tagged `{cut now | defer to Phase N | keep & defend | OPERATOR DECISION REQUIRED}` with a one-line rationale. This is the section the Reviewer adjudicates against; make it actionable, not abstract.
7. **Questions the Reviewer must put to the Operator** — the calls that are operator-authority, not yours and not the Reviewer's (e.g. "drop the Vault container for a Shamir-sealed master?", "collapse doctrine-only version bumps?", "defer the receipt-watcher to Phase 10?"). Frame each as a decision with its trade-off, not a recommendation.
8. **Auditor's blind spots** — what you deliberately did NOT assess and why, and where your confidence is low. Forced honesty about your own coverage so the Reviewer cannot be misled by your silence.
